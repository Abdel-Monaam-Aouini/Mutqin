# Evaluation results (synthetic, prototype)

Generated by `tools/prototype/evaluate.py`. **These numbers use a simulated acoustic model**
(`tools/prototype/acoustic_sim.py`): they show how the aligner behaves for a given phoneme error
rate and a stated assumption about its confidence scores, not how any real model performs.
Real-model rows will be added when a model is chosen.

Method: the recited phonemes (continuing or stopping reading per word, pauses as silence) are
turned into simulated frames, decoded with greedy CTC, aligned to the expected words with
word-level skip and repeat arcs, and judged per word (`tools/prototype/aligner.py`). A mistake
counts as found when an event of the right kind appears within one word of it. Injected wrong
and added words differ from their neighbours by at least half their phonemes; one-letter
differences are Phase 4 (letter-level) errors.

Seed 1, 150 trials per mistake kind, 300 correct recitations,
ranges of 1 to 3 ayahs. Targets (docs/PLAN.md): false alarms < 2%, mistake recall > 90%.
**Status:** false alarms meet the target (< 2%) at phoneme error rates up to 20% when the model's confidence is informative, and up to 11% otherwise. Recall of the right kind is above 90% only for skipped words; "wrong" and "added" words are found at about 80% (any kind), below the 90% target. Parameters were chosen to favour few false alarms over recall (CLAUDE.md rule 4). Tuning against a real model is still open.

Parameters: `Params(wrong_below=0.5, skip_aligned_max=0.25, low_conf=0.55, min_mismatch=2, repeat_min_ratio=0.75, skip_cost=1.5, repeat_cost=1.0, add_min_tokens=4, add_inside_min=3, max_jump_words=3, pause_frames=8)`.

## Real model: tarteel-ai/whisper-base-ar-quran (task 2.1, first candidate)

`tools/prototype/whisper_eval.py`: first 10 minutes (20 chunks of 30 s) of a professional,
correct recitation of surah 5, transcribed with greedy decoding and compared word by word to the
Tanzil simple-clean text of ayahs 1-60 (diacritics ignored; the model writes plain text).

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Words heard / reference words covered | 566 / 559 |
| Word error rate on a **correct** recitation | **10.2%** (26 substituted, 12 dropped, 19 inserted words) |
| Speed on the dev CPU | about 21 s per 30 s chunk (real-time factor 0.7, not streaming) |

Reading: every error here would be a false alarm for Mutqin, against a target below 2%. Errors
cluster where the reciter is fast or a word is rare (for example four consecutive words of
5:3 were replaced by unrelated words); Whisper also cannot see vowel or tajweed errors. It is not
usable as the mistake detector. The phoneme CTC candidates (Zipformer, wav2vec2) are still to be
measured the same way.

## Real model: TBOGamer22/wav2vec2-quran-phonetics (task 2.1, second candidate)

Apache-2.0, not gated, wav2vec2-base (95M parameters, 378 MB in fp32), greedy CTC. Qualitative check
only, on the first minute of the same surah 5 recitation in 15 s chunks. Findings:

- It was trained on **single-word clips** (Buraaq/quran-md-words), not connected recitation. On
  continuous recitation the output is mostly unreadable: the opening words "yā ayyuhā alladhīna
  āmanū awfū bil-ʿuqūd" came out as "bisidahihamhuhiyayhhnānūafūb'naūduḥil-nakūbahmatu...", with
  only fragments right and no word delimiters.
- Its vocabulary is a transliteration (ā ī ū, ʿ, ṭ ...), not our 31 phonemes, so a mapping would be
  needed; its vowels do not mark shaddah or tajweed.
- It is not streaming (full-attention wav2vec2). Speed is good (about 1 s per 15 s on the dev CPU).

Not usable for ayah-level tracking as released. It could only serve for single-word clips.

## Desktop speed (task 3.7, pieces measured; full-model figure pending)

Release build, AMD Ryzen 7 5800H (16 threads), one thread used. Real-time factor (RTF) =
processing time / audio time; lower is faster. Reproduce: `-DMUTQIN_BUILD_BENCH=ON`, run
`mutqin_bench 120`, and `mutqin-cli ... --posteriors FILE --chunk N`.

| Stage | Measurement | RTF |
|---|---|---|
| Log-mel features | 120 s of audio in 0.27 s | 0.0022 |
| Energy VAD | 120 s in 1.6 ms | 0.00001 |
| Aligner + detector, whole 167 s recitation of 2:6-40 (700+ words) pushed at once | 214 ms | 0.0013 |
| same, pushed every 250 ms (668 pushes) | 766 ms total | 0.0046 |
| same, pushed every 40 ms (4,175 pushes) | 2.97 s total, 0.7 ms per push | 0.018 |
| same, pushed every 10 ms (16,699 pushes) | 10.7 s total, 0.64 ms per push | 0.064 |
| sherpa-onnx backend with a public int8 streaming Zipformer CTC test model (Chinese, 5.6 s and 1.8 s clips) | 0.24 s and 0.08 s | 0.04 |

Reading: the front end and the aligner cost almost nothing next to a model; even pushing every
10 ms keeps the aligner far below the 0.3 target. The test model is not our phoneme model (the
Zipformer from the candidate list is 65M parameters, similar in size to this one), so the model
row is indicative only; the real figure and the latency to a reported mistake need the real model.
Phone benchmarks are deferred to Phase 3b.

## Real model: Muno459/zipformer_p-quran (task 2.1, third candidate, first run 2026-10-14)

Streaming Zipformer2 CTC, 65.5M parameters, int8 ONNX (73 MB), run through sherpa-onnx (greedy). Its output
is 250 phoneme units; the aligner here compares the units heard against the model's own canonical
phonemes of each word (`ordered_quran_phonemes.json`, shipped with the model), unchanged aligner and
default parameters (`tools/prototype/zipformer_eval.py`). The recording is a professional, correct
recitation of surah 5 (49 minutes, 120 ayahs, 2,444 words judged), so every flagged word is a false alarm.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Flagged words (wrong or skipped) on correct recitation | **44 of 2,444 = 1.8%** (first 10 minutes: 4 of 487 = 0.8%); target < 2% |
| All events including repeated / added | 83 (3.4% of words): 34 wrong, 30 repeated, 10 skipped, 9 added |
| Speed (4 threads, dev CPU) | real-time factor 0.031 (49 min in 90 s) |
| For comparison, Whisper base on the same file | 10.2% word errors |

What the flags look like: most are the **first word of an ayah** where the first one or two units are
missing or attached to the previous word (expected `يَ اااا ءَ ييُ هَ`, heard `ييُ هَ`), plus a few real
recognition errors. Not yet measured: recall on injected mistakes, other reciters, learners' recitation,
phone microphones. The reciter's madd lengths differ from the model's fixed canonical lengths (4 counts),
so long-vowel units can differ by length without being a mistake.

### Tuning on the real model's output (2026-10-14)

`tools/prototype/zipformer_tune.py` reuses the real units heard on the 49-minute recording. False alarms: the
recording is tiled into windows of 50 groups and only interior words are scored (a window cuts the audio
mid-word at both ends). Recall: one mistake is injected into the real heard units of a window (skipped: the
word's units removed; wrong: replaced by another word's units; repeated: the previous two words heard
again; added: an unrelated word's units inserted), 100 trials per kind, found if reported within one word.

| Settings | Flagged words (clean) | All events (clean) | Skipped | Wrong (kind / any) | Repeated | Added (kind / any) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| defaults of the simulation | 1.7% | 3.3% | 100% | 80% / 99% | 97% | 74% / 79% |
| + up to 2 missing units at each word edge (`edge_free=2`) | 0.7% | 2.3% | 100% | 77% / 96% | 97% | 74% / 79% |
| + long runs of one character cut to 2 (madd length ignored) | 0.6% | 2.2% | 100% | 77% / 96% | 99% | 73% / 78% |
| + added word from 3 units (`add_min_tokens=3`) | **0.6%** | **2.2%** | 100% | 77% / 96% | 99% | **89% / 94%** |

Chosen for the unit-based path: `edge_free=2`, run cap 2, `add_min_tokens=3`. Caveats: one reciter, one
recitation style, professional audio; injected mistakes are synthetic (one per window, units only); learners,
other reciters and phone microphones are not measured. "Events" counts every reported event including
repeated / added; the 0.6% counts only wrong or skipped words.

### End to end through the C++ core and `mutqin-cli` (2026-10-14)

`mutqin-cli --surah 5 --ayah 1-8 --model models/zipformer_p-quran/mutqin_model.txt --wav FILE`, first 100 s of
the same recitation (it starts with the basmala), edited with ffmpeg using the model's own word timestamps:

| Audio | Reported (besides 2 events that also appear on the unedited audio) |
|---|---|
| unedited | `repeated 5:2:14`, `wrong 5:3:25` (2 events in 100 s) |
| word 5:1:6 cut out | `skipped 5:1:6` |
| word 5:1:7 cut out | `skipped 5:1:7` |
| words 5:1:12-13 said twice | `repeated 5:1:13` |
| word 5:1:15 replaced by another word's audio | `wrong 5:1:15` |
| another word's audio inserted after 5:1:15 | `added 5:1:15` |

Each edit is reported at the right place. A first cut of the 3-unit word 5:1:14 left a one-unit
fragment and was not reported: a fragment of a short word is treated as a pronunciation variant (at most a
third of a word may be missing at its edges). Whole run of 100 s: about 6 s on 4 threads including the start-up.
`core/tests/test_zipformer.cpp` repeats the clean and the cut-word run (skipped unless the model is available).

### A second reciter: Husary, 339 single-ayah clips (2026-10-14)

`tools/prototype/zipformer_clips.py`: EveryAyah recordings of Husary (a different voice and a slow murattal
style, research use only, see `LICENSES.md`), surahs 1, 2 (part), 10 (part), 60-64 and 105-114, one clip per ayah, 175 minutes in
all, 4,744 words judged. Tuned profile, expected units of that one ayah.

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Flagged words (wrong or skipped) | **5 of 4,744 = 0.1%** |
| All events | 51 = 1.1% (5 wrong, 45 repeated, 1 added) |
| Clips with an event | 38 of 339 |

The 45 "repeated" events are not false alarms: listening to what the model heard, the reciter really says
some phrases twice at the breath points of long ayahs (for example 10:24 and 2:177 contain a doubled
phrase). Changing the repeat cost or the repeat ratio leaves these events unchanged, which fits: they match
too well to be noise. Together with the 49-minute recitation of surah 5 this gives two voices and two styles
below the 2% target; learners' recitation and phone microphones are still unmeasured.

### Latency to an event while streaming (2026-10-14)

`mutqin-cli --wav FILE --timeline` pushes the audio in 100 ms chunks, as a microphone would, and prints each
event with the audio time at which it first appeared. Edited 100 s clip of 5:1-8 (the model's look-ahead is
about 1 s; the core does not judge the word being spoken until the next word has started):

| Mistake | Where it happened in the audio | Event first shown | Delay after the end of the faulty word |
|---|---|---|---|
| word 5:1:6 cut out | the next word 5:1:7 spans 8.6-10.1 s | 10.3 s (`skipped 5:1:6`) | about 0.2 s after the next word ended |
| words 5:1:12-13 said twice | repeat ends at 17.15 s | 18.0 s (`repeated 5:1:13`) | 0.9 s |
| word 5:1:15 replaced | ends at 16.98 s | 18.4 s (`wrong 5:1:15`) | 1.4 s |
| word added after 5:1:15 | ends at 17.4 s | 19.4 s (`added 5:1:15`) | 2.0 s |

So a mistake is reported about 1-2 s after it is spoken. Before the commit rule (the last word heard is not
judged) the same run showed 20-30 transient false events per clip that later disappeared; now only the
two events that the unedited audio also has (`repeated 5:2:14` at 37 s, `wrong 5:3:25` at 100 s) and the edit
itself appear. The target of 300 ms in `PLAN.md` is not met by this model (its own look-ahead is 1 s);
whether 1-2 s is acceptable for the user experience is an open decision.

### Strictness levels (2026-10-14)

The app offers three levels (`mq_session_set_strictness`, `mutqin-cli --strictness`, `make live LEVEL=strict`).
They are parameter sets of the same aligner, measured like the tuning above on the real model's output
(`zipformer_tune.py` with 100 injected mistakes per kind in the 49-minute recording; `zipformer_clips.py` on the
339 clips of the second reciter). Standard is the profile tuned before; the other two were chosen from a small
grid (profiles with `edge_free` 0 to 3, `add_min_tokens` 2 to 4, `wrong_below` 0.4 to 0.6).

| Level | `edge_free` | `add_min_tokens` | `wrong_below` | `min_mismatch` | Flagged words, surah 5 | Events, surah 5 | Flagged words, Husary clips | Skipped | Wrong (any kind) | Repeated | Added (any kind) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| relaxed | 3 | 4 | 0.4 | 3 | 0.5% | 2.1% | 0.0% | 100% | 87% | 99% | 77% |
| standard (default) | 2 | 3 | 0.5 | 2 | 0.9% | 2.5% | 0.1% | 100% | 97% | 99% | 94% |
| strict | 1 | 2 | 0.6 | 2 | 1.2% | 3.3% | 0.3% | 100% | 99% | 99% | 97% |

Relaxed stays well below the false-alarm target but no longer reaches 90% recall for wrong and added words;
strict keeps flagged words under 2% and finds almost everything, at the price of more stray events. The most
sensitive profile tried (no edge tolerance) reached 100% / 97% recall but flagged 2.0% of words, so it was not
offered. The same profiles in Python (`STRICTNESS_PROFILES`) and C++ (`StrictnessProfile`) are checked against
each other by the aligner parity test. Through the C++ core on the first 100 s of the recording: the clean audio
gives 1 / 2 / 4 events at relaxed / standard / strict, and a replaced word is missed at relaxed and found at the
other two. Levels apply to the Zipformer's units only; the built-in phonemizer text has one profile.

| Check | Result |

More threads do not help on this phone (the 8 cores are a mix of fast and slow ones), so 2 threads is the sensible
default there. Not measured: battery and heat over a 30-minute session, a microphone session with the phone's
own audio path, other phones, and old devices. The timings are for pushing the file as fast as possible; the
1-2 s delay to a reported mistake in live use does not depend on the compute speed as long as the factor is
below 1.

Finding from this run: the first resampler spent a sine and a cosine per filter tap and cost about 10 s per 100 s of
44.1 kHz audio on the phone (real-time factor about 0.1). It now uses a precomputed filter table and costs about
2 s (0.02); on the desktop it is 0.004 (`mutqin_bench`).

## Sliding window for long ranges (synthetic only)

A range of more than 3000 expected units is aligned through a window of about 2000 units that moves with the
reciter (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`). Measured with synthetic posteriors built from the expected words of 2:1-60
(5442 units, the window moves twice): the verdicts and events are identical to one alignment over the whole
range, for a clean recitation, for words skipped early and late, and for a repeated word near the end; a change
of the feedback level judges everything again (`core/tests/test_session_window.cpp`). Peak memory of that test
process (Quran text, phonemizer and the 5442-unit session): 58 MB. Not measured yet: real recordings of a long
recitation, speed and memory on a phone, and whole al-Baqarah (24500 units).
